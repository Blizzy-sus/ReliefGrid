# ReliefGrid: scenario results

Generated by `npm run scenarios` (fixed clock and seeds, so results are reproducible). **Coverage** = (units already allocated + units allocated now) ÷ approved units. 
*Jain index is a standard fairness score for coverage ratios: 1.00 = everyone has the same coverage. ReliefGrid deliberately trades some of it for priority, so read it together with the Critical and worst-off columns.

## 1. Abundant stock

Stock 20,000 L, demand 15,500 L. Scarce: **false**. Allocations: Riverside School 6,000/6,000, Temple Hall 2,500/2,500, Stadium 7,000/7,000.

When nothing is short, priority is irrelevant and everyone is served in full.

First-come-first-served gives the long-waiting low-urgency hostel everything it asked for while the **Critical** camp, which asked last, gets nothing. Equal sharing ignores the emergency entirely. Strict priority serves the critical camp fully but leaves the three lowest-priority camps with nothing. ReliefGrid gives the critical camp the largest share of what it needs, gives every camp something, and rewards camps that have waited long.

## 4. Over-claiming is capped by a planning standard

| Camp population | Standard (per person/day) | Requested | Approved | Outcome |
| :-- | --: | --: | --: | --: |
| 1,000 | 5 | 40,000 | 15,000 | trimmed |
| 1,000 | 5 | 15,000 | 15,000 | as requested |
| 200 | 0.5 | 900 | 300 | trimmed |
| 300 | none | 500 | 500 | as requested |

A camp cannot inflate its share by asking for more than the standard allows. Items with no standard (tarpaulins, insulin) are approved as requested.

## 5. Fairness across successive rounds (deficit awareness)

Two camps with identical priority each request 1,000 units. Round 1: 400 units arrive but only camp A has a request open, so A receives all 400. Round 2: camp B has now asked too and 600 more units arrive.

| Camp | Already holds | Naive: share of remaining need | Cumulative coverage (naive) | ReliefGrid (deficit-aware) | Cumulative coverage (ReliefGrid) |
| :-- | --: | --: | --: | --: | --: |
| A (served in round 1) | 400 | 225 | 63% | 100 | 50% |
| B (asks in round 2) | 0 | 375 | 38% | 500 | 50% |

A method that only looks at remaining need gives camp A another 225 units although it already holds 40%, ending at 63% for A against 38% for B. ReliefGrid brings both camps to the same cumulative coverage (50%): A +100, B +500.

## 6. Waiting is rewarded, so low urgency is not starved forever

| Request | Urgency weight | Vulnerability | Aging | Priority | Allocated of 1,000 | Coverage |
| :-- | --: | --: | --: | --: | --: | --: |
| Low urgency, waited 72h | 1 | 1.20 | 1.50 | 1.80 | 300 | 30% |
| Low urgency, just asked | 1 | 1.20 | 1.00 | 1.20 | 200 | 20% |
| Moderate, just asked | 2 | 1.20 | 1.00 | 2.40 | 400 | 40% |

Aging (up to +50% after 48 h) lets a request that has waited three days overtake an identical fresh one, but it does not overturn a genuine difference in urgency.
